## 1. The changed completion contract

The product milestone is a person understanding the relevant coverage paths, comparing actual options on supported dimensions, seeing what could change the answer, and receiving a recommendation only when the evidence supports its stated scope. Education and useful comparison are valuable outputs before a recommendation is possible.

Full L03 clause-by-clause completion and a complete whole-offering pilot are **no longer prerequisites for every first product comparison or recommendation**. They remain separate, unfinished research objectives. This explicitly supersedes the conflicting critical-path requirement in the earlier D77/D87 whole-offering milestones; it does not redefine those milestones as complete.

Relevance is established at benefit-family and decision-dependency level, with a mandatory check for shared deductibles, limits, exclusions and other cross-benefit qualifiers. Retained known disputes and gaps must be checked against the selected families and dependencies; unrelated material may remain in the research backlog.

## 2. What useful means in an educational tool

The first experience should answer five questions in ordinary language:

1. Which coverage paths am I considering, and what are the consequential tradeoffs?
2. What do these actual plans charge, and under what circumstances?
3. What is established about my doctors, medicines, travel and expressed needs?
4. Why might I prefer one option, and what would change that conclusion?
5. What remains to check before deciding or taking action?

Teach premium versus service cost, deductible versus spending limit, network versus provider identity, covered benefit versus approval/payment, and fixed cost versus scenario estimate when those distinctions matter. Explain conditions next to the affected result. Retain deeper source detail and contextual visuals on request. Preserve the adaptive curriculum and task return point; no mandatory lesson sequence, quiz, second questionnaire or requirement to answer all 41 meanings. A delivered explanation is not evidence of comprehension.

Represent Original Medicare with optional Medigap/separate Part D and Medicare Advantage fairly before policy choice where relevant. The first real same-role implementation uses retained MA data. It must not imply that an MA-only selection establishes the best coverage path or a complete Medicare market. Personal Medigap quotes, complete payer coordination, exact enrollment-right findings and enrollment remain separate capabilities. Current coverage, keep and defer remain visible outcomes, with their own unresolved transition checks.

## 3. Evidence depth and output boundaries

| Output | Required evidence | Useful result when something is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General education | Reviewed concept/rule wording at its proper year and scope | Explain the concept without a personal eligibility, price or fit assertion |
| Actual published plan facts | Exact offering/year/county, applicable published value/conditions and accepted display purpose | Display supported cells and a concrete unknown; no fabricated zero or implicit completeness |
| Personal comparison | Relevant reports, exact item identities, reviewed applicable findings/methods and accepted comparison purpose | Compare supported dimensions and identify what prevents a conclusion on others |
| Scoped recommendation | Confirmed decision-input summary, resolved material constraints, supported tradeoffs, reviewed recommendation method, candidate-universe statement and exact-purpose acceptance | Return comparison/research next steps when a missing fact could change the recommendation |
| Scenario cost calculation | Declared utilization, complete rules for included components, applicable limits/deductibles and reviewed calculation method | Show components or a labeled subtotal; omit unsupported totals and savings claims |

An output cannot escape recommendation requirements by calling itself educational or provisional. A claim that an option fits this person better receives the recommendation gate even inside teaching prose.

Mandatory screening covers identity/year/geography, coverage category and restrictions, baseline/transition hazards, core financial exposure and person-relevant care access. Missing material information holds the dependent conclusion. Unknown does not mean poor fit or exclusion. Absence of a stated concern does not establish that a general deductible, network restriction or major service cost is irrelevant.

Examples: missing optional dental procedure frequencies hold a precise dental claim, and may hold a recommendation for a person planning that procedure. They need not hold an independently supported plan-premium comparison. A disputed hospital charging unit holds a hospital scenario and any conclusion relying on it. A provider identity match alone cannot establish participation or appointment availability. An unverified eligibility condition prevents saying the person can enroll, while correctly scoped catalog education can continue.

Do not treat a footnote as irrelevant because it is inconvenient. Material dependencies include shared budgets, benefit-period resets, service/network distinctions, selective deductibles, restrictions, exclusions and exceptions affecting the claim. Global exclusions are screened for applicability to the selected benefits rather than universally rewritten into structured fields.

## 6. Comparison and recommendation method

1. Establish the complete declared candidate scope and current-coverage baseline. A three-plan validation set is labeled three selected plans, never the complete Collier market. Engineering test selection cannot become a personalized shortlist. A recommendation within a small set requires either the person explicitly requesting comparison of those exact plans or a reviewed, neutral candidate-selection method that accounts for the relevant universe and visible unresolved alternatives. Otherwise the three-plan output remains an educational comparison. Screen the retained 72-offering MA universe for the later catalog milestone; account separately for PDP/Medigap and restricted options.
2. Build the same dimension matrix for each compared unit. For each need, retain `supports`, `conflicts` or `unresolved` with exact conditions and evidence. Listing a doctor or priority does not automatically create a hard constraint.
3. Evaluate explicit constraints. Keep known conflicts and unresolved candidates inspectable; a supported user-controlled filter may hide known conflicts with counts and a reversible explanation. Never reward data completeness as plan suitability.
4. Explain supported tradeoffs using comparable values. With unordered priorities, present alternatives and their different advantages. With an explicit ordered priority and accepted policy, a transparent lexicographic comparison may support a scoped recommendation. No invented numerical fit score, probability, weights or assumed annual utilization.
5. Check sensitivity: would an unresolved material field, plausible declared scenario or changed priority alter the recommendation? If yes, narrow the claim or hold the recommendation. If source evidence supplies defensible bounds, use a reviewed bound-based method; never invent a range to get past an unknown.
6. Return reasons, important drawbacks, alternatives, current/keep/defer context, scope limits and the next useful check. Unknown potentially superior alternatives prevent an all-market “best” claim. No unique supported preference means no forced winner.

The initial recommendation can legitimately identify multiple suitable alternatives within an explicitly evaluated scope. Enrollment eligibility, affordability, medical appropriateness and a binding quote require their own evidence; recommendation wording must not imply those findings.
