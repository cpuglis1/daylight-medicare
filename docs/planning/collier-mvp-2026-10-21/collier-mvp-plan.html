UX design

  • Ask what changes the next step: location, coverage/timing, medicines, doctors, pharmacy and goals. Explain why; preserve unknown/edit paths and the experienced shortcut.
  • Teach one concept at a time, starting with Medicare. Pair each question with a visual; keep one Continue, explicit Back/help, and entered answers/notes.
  • Test with adults 65+ on desktop, tablet and phone against WCAG 2.2 AA and W3C older-user/COGA guidance: readable type/contrast, generous targets, keyboard/zoom and clear labels.

Outcome Observed comprehension and task completion, with remaining usability gaps recorded.

03

LLM Component

  • Choose the model through grounded Medicare scenarios: compare accuracy, plain language, latency, cost and safe fallback. Reuse existing evaluations before changing the current choice.
  • Select app and model hosting for reliability, privacy/log retention, operating cost and ownership. Keep verified facts/calculations outside generated prose; disclose unavailable live help.

Outcome A documented model/hosting decision with measured tradeoffs and operating limits.

04

Plans

  • Refine the existing dashboard/grid for both coverage paths: easy browse/filter/compare, key costs and coverage, eligibility conditions, sources and unresolved checks.
  • Define and test ranking inputs, weights and tie rules. Explain “why this order.” Distinguish published-field sorting from personal fit; exclude unsupported facts from scoring.

Outcome A legible shortlist whose order and tradeoffs a person can explain.

05

HITL · closing handoff

  • Define text → human closing after selection. Text assistance confirms the choice and open checks; a licensed human confirms facts, final quote and any enrollment through an authorized channel.
  • Obtain consent before sharing. Transfer only necessary context; show the receiving contact, handoff status and next step.

Outcome A consented, traceable handoff. Selecting a plan never implies enrollment.
