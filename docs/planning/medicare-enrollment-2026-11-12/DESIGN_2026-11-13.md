**Two changes to the wording Chris suggested**, and why:

- He wrote "…looking at Medigap options, not Medicare Advantage". I dropped "not Medicare Advantage". It closes a door this person opened in their first sentence.
- He wrote "…since these plans may align better to your priorities". I dropped it. That is Daylight judging fit in a sentence; the card already gives the reason in the person's own words ("You said predictable costs matter more").
- His note for the teaching beat said "you pay more of course". That is true of Medigap's monthly premium. It is not true of many Medicare Advantage plans, which is why the card states each side's cost separately.

**The teaching beat (comment 5)** is the top line and the left column of this card: what Original Medicare alone leaves a person paying, and what people add. It is not a separate turn.

## 3. The phone guide uses what the person told Daylight

A block at the top of the phone guide, "For your call", built by code from the person's reports. A line appears only when its fact was reported. With nothing reported the guide is as today.

For the person in Chris's test (turned 65 October 20, coverage through his own current job, retiring at year end):

```
For your call

Say
  "I turned 65 on October 20, 2026."
  "I have health coverage through my current job. I plan to retire at the end of the year."
  "I'd like to check when I can sign up for Medicare and when it would start."     (existing line)

Ask
  "When should I sign up for Part B so it starts when my job coverage ends?"
  "Do I need my employer to fill in form CMS-L564?"

Good to know before you call
  A person covered by a group health plan through their own or their spouse's current work may qualify
  to enroll in Part B while covered or during an eight-month Special Enrollment Period after employment
  or that coverage ends, whichever ends first. COBRA does not extend that window.          (accepted)
  The federal Medigap Open Enrollment Period lasts six months starting the first month a person is at
  least 65 and enrolled in Part B. Starting Part B while still working can start this clock; retirement
  does not automatically restart it.                                                        (accepted)
```

Other situations use the same pattern:

| Reported | "Say" and "Ask" lines |
|---|---|
| Turning 65, neither part, no job coverage | The existing line: "I want to sign up for Medicare Part A and Part B." |
| Has Part A, wants Part B | The existing line, plus "Do I need form CMS-L564?" only when job coverage was reported |
| Already applied | "I applied on [the date they told Daylight]. Can you tell me where it stands?" |
| Helping someone else | Lines say "my mother" (or the relationship given) and add "I'm calling with her beside me." |

It never shows or asks for a Social Security number or Medicare number. The existing line stays: have the number ready for Social Security only.

**Needs from Codex.** The date job coverage ends, as a report. The person said "retiring at EOY" and Daylight kept nothing it could use; employment dates are held scope today. Without it the second "Say" line stops after "current job".

## 5. The closing summary needs a rule first (C33)

Chris wants the conversation to end with the top options and why, then natural next steps. Before any design, six questions. My suggested answer follows each.

1. **Which plan letter for "predictable costs"?** Suggested: Plan G first, as the most complete letter someone new to Medicare can buy, with Plan N beside it as the lower-premium option. Both plan-letter lines are already signed off.
2. **Which company?** Suggested: the lowest listed premium for that letter at the person's age, sex category, tobacco category and ZIP. Say "lowest listed premium", never "best". Commission never enters.
3. **Part D.** Suggested: always address it (Medigap has no drug coverage; the penalty sentence is accepted), but name a plan only when the medicine check against each drug list is running. Today it is not.
4. **The doctor line.** For Medigap: "accepts Medicare", from the doctor card. "In network" only for a Medicare Advantage plan that was actually checked.
5. **The word.** "Recommend" or "your top options for what you told me"? Suggested: the second, until the enrollment and FMO questions (C32) are settled.
6. **Next steps offered.** Suggested buttons: See these plans · What is Plan G? · What about Medicare Advantage? · How Medigap fits with Original Medicare · Do I need Part D?
