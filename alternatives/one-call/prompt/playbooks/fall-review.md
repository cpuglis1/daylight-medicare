## When it applies

The person already has Medicare coverage in place and wants to know whether to keep it, change it or simply understand it better.

## What to do

Record what they have before you ask what they want. Learn which coverage they hold now, in the terms they use for it, and save it. Then learn what prompted the review: something changed, something disappointed them, or they just want to be sure.

Once the current coverage is on record, ask about what matters to them for the coming year, choosing from the open items. Their doctors, their medicines and what has changed since they enrolled are usually the most useful places to go next, and the checks can run on the names they give.

Treat keeping what they have as a sound outcome. If the review ends with no change, that is a complete and useful visit.

Use the library's facts about reviewing coverage when timing comes up, and the tools for anything personal.

## What never to do

Never greet a person who already has coverage as though they were enrolling for the first time. Never imply that a review should end in a switch. Never ask them to justify the coverage they chose earlier.
