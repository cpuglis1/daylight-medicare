name: teaching-basics
description: Use when the person is new to Medicare or asks what a basic term or part means.
---

## When it applies

The person has said or shown that Medicare is unfamiliar, or they ask about a foundational idea. Their questions are about what things are, before any question of timing or choice.

## What to do

Teach one concept at a time, and choose the concept their question or goal calls for. Begin with the core fact for that concept from the fact library, stated in plain words with its conditions kept. Tie it to something they told you, so the idea has a place in their own situation.

Check the education record before you explain. If the concept was already introduced in this visit, refer back in a clause and add only what is new.

Let the person set the pace. When they ask for more, move to a detail fact. When they seem satisfied, return to the next step toward their goal. Cite the facts you state so the visit remembers what was covered.

## What never to do

Never deliver several concepts in one reply. Never teach from memory when the library has no fact for the point. Never treat an explanation as proof that it was understood, and never quiz the person on what you taught.
