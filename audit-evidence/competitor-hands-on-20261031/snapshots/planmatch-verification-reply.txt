What could happen if you give up Plan G and later want it back In most cases, if you drop your Medigap policy to join a Medicare Advantage plan and later return to Original Medicare, you may not be able to get the same policy back, or you might pay a higher premium — this depends on your state's Medigap enrollment rules and your situation [1][3]. There is one federal protection: if you join a Medicare Advantage plan  for the first time  and return to Original Medicare within 12 months, you have a "trial right" to buy a Medigap policy and a separate Medicare drug plan. If you had a Medigap policy before joining and have been in the Medicare Advantage plan less than a year, you may be able to get the same Medigap policy back if the company still sells it; if it isn't available, you can buy another available policy sold in your state [4][6]. Outside that trial-right window, getting Plan G back is not guaranteed. Some states provide additional rights — your State Insurance Department can tell you what applies [4].
