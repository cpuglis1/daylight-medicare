What the live tests establish about overlap

  • Education plus plan browsing already exists. PlanMatch’s public Ramona gave relevant coverage-path education with sources and explicit unknowns, alongside actual 2027 plan listings. It expressly declined specific recommendations; the chat could not substantiate the personal drug/provider checks.
  • Plan results without a sales call already exist. MatchYourMedicare and MedicareShopper returned named plans and annual estimates. That establishes useful self-service competition, not validated personal total-cost accuracy. Their leading plans and near-identical estimates were similar; a shared data engine is only a hypothesis.
  • Recommendation explanations can overstate the research performed. MedicareShopper’s card asserted a network match with no named doctors entered. Hughes and Healix supplied plan/drug assertions without the exact evidence needed to verify them. These are observed verification gaps, not proof every named assertion was false.
  • Coverage-path decisions need scrutiny. Healthpilot and MedicareShopper steered this travel-and-doctor-freedom case toward MA; Harbor’s separate personalization branch shifted toward supplements. A single case cannot establish systematic bias or which path is personally correct.
  • Daylight’s differentiation is a demanding execution standard. The intended combination is contextual teaching, fair path comparison, verified plan/drug/provider research, inspectable cost assumptions, commercial independence and useful progress without a mandatory call. This session did not establish that any tested product completed that entire chain.

The common case and what remains unknown

This is a fictional research test, not a real person or enrollment request. I am a 67-year-old woman in Naples, Florida, ZIP 34102, Collier County, born April 15, 1959. I have Medicare A and B, Medigap Plan G and a standalone Part D plan. I am reviewing coverage for 2027. I take metformin 500 mg twice daily (60 tablets/month), lisinopril 10 mg daily (30/month), and atorvastatin 20 mg daily (30/month), filled at CVS. I have diabetes and high blood pressure, no Medicaid or Extra Help, and do not smoke. I spend four months a year in New Jersey and want routine care there as well as in Florida. Help me understand keeping Original Medicare plus G and Part D versus switching to Medicare Advantage before recommending policies. Then show actual local options and estimated total annual costs, tell me what you checked and what remains unknown, and let me continue without a sales call.

Name, where a form requested one: Pat Morgan. Forms received supported fields rather than invented substitutes. Preferences favored predictable costs, doctor freedom and domestic travel; diabetes and hypertension were entered, and extras were low priority. Drug strength alone was not treated as evidence of dose, quantity or pharmacy verification.

Current G/PDP carrier and premiums, exact doctors, Medicare eligibility date, prior MA history and underwriting facts were intentionally unresolved. A defensible keep-versus-switch cost comparison needs additional information. The profile is an experienced beneficiary, so this test does not establish beginner teaching quality. The single case is neither an exhaustive market study nor a repeatability/adversarial evaluation.

No login account, paid subscription, phone call, microphone permission, callback request, enrollment or medical order was created. No real user health records were connected. Contact-gated results were left untested.

Questions to use in a manual demo

  1. Can I use this fictional case in a sandbox without becoming a sales lead?
  2. Will it fairly compare retaining Original Medicare plus G plus Part D against MA before choosing individual policies?
  3. Can it show the precise year, contract/PBP, plan universe, exclusions and source date?
  4. What exact doses, monthly quantities, CVS location and Florida/New Jersey providers did it actually verify?
  5. What is included in annual cost: premiums, drugs, expected medical use, travel/out-of-network exposure and the current-coverage baseline?
  6. What facts are missing, and will it ask rather than silently filling them in?
  7. Can I inspect alternatives and keep current coverage, without a call or an enrollment?
  8. Do commercial relationships affect inclusion, ordering or explanations, and what evidence supports the answer?

For exact medical/coverage rules, independently check official sources such as Medicare Plan Compare and Medicare’s coverage-path comparison. This handoff evaluates observed product behavior; it does not certify plan facts or legal rights.
